Approach and implementation

To begin the week, I reviewed and merged my lab partner's code into the repository. The most important change I made while reviewing was making an adjustment to how the model was being used to make predictions. The adjustment consisted of changing the use of the keras model predict() method to __call__(), as this magic method is far more efficient for small inputs, this resulted in a speed-up from ~15 minutes of execution time to ~1 minute.

Next, I transitioned to implementing the deletion of nodes within the neural network. In the original repository, the authors chose to just zero out node weights and biases, effectively deleting them. However, for efficiency concerns in our project, we must actually delete the nodes. To do this, I wrote a script to parse through layer weight and bias matrices and remove nodes that are zeroed out and their corresponding input rows for the next layer. Next, the script iteratively builds a new model using new weight matrices.

Lastly, I reviewed the comments that the ISSTA reviewers left on our submission. Ultimately, our paper was rejected for publication, but reviewing the comments gave important insights that I can use in future work. Overall, the experience of writing a research paper was a great learning experience.
