Approach and implementation

On Tuesday and Thursday I met with my lab to discuss the next project we are working on. The goal of this project is to explore the possibility of storing neural network training data in MPEG format to accelerate the training process. The key insight of using MPEG is the valuable compression it offers, allowing us to reduce the size of training datasets and more effectively store it in vram.

The MPEG training acceleration project has already had some progress before I joined it, so my first task was to replicate the results up till now. To do this I cloned the project repository into my WSL(Windows Subsystem for Linux) environment. Next I ran the needed tests and documented the results in a excel spreadsheet to share with the team.

To assist with my understanding of the project I spent time this week researching MPEG compression. Taking notes on key topics such as spatial compression, temporal compression, I-frames,P-frames, and B-frames. This research has allowed me to have a solid understanding of MPEGs.

Lastly, I spent the end of the week researching how the lab can implement random sampling for MPEG videos. This is important for our problem because we need to make sure that our MPEG videos do not introduce bias during the training process of a model, and a way to combat this is random sampling from the dataset. The current solution that I found is using the framework FFmpeg. I will present this to my mentor in the next weekly meeting.
